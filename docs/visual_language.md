# Visual language

White Line uses an editorial diagram language rather than an evidentiary
dashboard. The page is warm cream paper; black contours mark the boundary of a
claim; teal, ochre, and deep purple distinguish the three kinds of absence and
the state/protocol paths. Curved, slightly irregular lines keep the diagrams
from implying that an open question is a machine-complete object.

The visual system has three jobs:

1. **Orient.** The cover art makes the central reading rule felt before the
   formal method: an open field can hold a boundary without becoming a hidden
   explanation. The scholarship matrix then makes the translation accountable
   by showing the question each literature forces and the limit of the code's
   response.
2. **Explain.** The review atlas and state diagrams show the typed pipeline,
   caution order, and bounded follow-up without inventing observations. Two
   further plates show a contract being exercised rather than described: the
   reason trail draws the ordered reasons one live assessment wrote for each
   branch of the scoring rule, and the invariant battery draws the detail string
   each structural check returned when handed a registry built to defeat it.
   Both are the return values of calls made at build time, so a reworded reason
   or a weakened check moves the plate.
3. **Constrain.** Captions and in-figure labels repeat the non-claim: a gap is
   not evidence of a hidden cause, a higher caution rank is not a danger score,
   and a follow-up action is not an approval.

Colour is always a second channel, never the only one. Each kind carries a
marker shape as well as a hue — circle, square, triangle — and every panel
names its kind in words, so the figures read in greyscale and under colour
vision differences. That contract only holds if the words can actually be
read, which for a long time they could not: the shipped plates bottomed out at
3.39 points because a height cap shrank the tall ones. Two mechanisms hold the
floor now. `canvas.text` refuses any label below `MIN_TEXT_SIZE`, and
`tests/test_figure_legibility.py` re-derives the printed point size of the
smallest label in every plate and fails below six points. A canvas may
therefore grow taller but never wider than `MAX_CANVAS_WIDTH`: width is what
sets the scale on the page.

All figures are deterministic SVG compositions generated by
`white_line.figures.build_figures`; `scripts/build_figures.py` is its command
line entry point. The operational diagrams derive from the live
registry, state alphabet, and review protocol; the scholarship matrix also
declares its manuscript synthesis as a source. They are rasterized to PNG for
the manuscript and accompanied by their SVG source and figure-registry
metadata. When developing a visual,
generate it into a disposable preview root under `~/Downloads/`, inspect the
PNG at full size, and only then promote the exact generated artifacts to
`output/figures/`. The cover art is charcoal-like by construction—layered
graphite paths, an irregular aperture, and restrained sienna/purple traces—so
it remains reproducible while carrying more emotional weight than a dashboard.
This keeps visual judgment separate from the reproducible
source-to-artifact chain.
