# Relationship to the line set {#sec:line-set-relationship}

White Line is the absence work among eight peer color instruments, whose shared
framing is declared by the colorless wrapper
[`line_set`](https://github.com/docxology/line_set). The original four were Red
(refusal and personal security boundaries), Black (disciplined work), Golden
(aspiration and direction), and White (absence, withholding, and epistemic
restraint). Silver adds memory, custody, succession, and deliberate lapse/release;
Violet records affected-party consent without inferring missing consent; Blue
addresses ongoing stewardship; Green addresses capacity under cultivation,
not competence certification. These are distinct native instruments, not
comparable grades.

The wrapper is outside the color set and adds no substantive instrument.
[Witness Register](https://github.com/docxology/witness_register) co-registers
reports and relations without aggregation and is also not a color. Membership
does not establish source installation or access: optional or access-controlled
sources may be unavailable in a particular environment. White Line copies no
sibling registries or evaluators and imports, depends on, and defers to none of
them. A gap or restraint is never evidence that an unseen cause exists.

## Note on the name

The original four line works — Black, White, Golden, Red — carry colors that openly echo
the stages of the alchemical *magnum opus*, and White Line stands for **Albedo**,
the whitening. Read only in Carl Jung's symbolic-psychological register of
individuation, albedo is the washing that follows the blackening: a figure for
purification, clarification, and cleared negative space — never an empirical,
mystical, or causal event. The emblem fits this instrument precisely because it
asserts nothing; whitening here is restraint and the discipline of the unwritten,
not a sign that some absent or hidden thing is real, safe, or at work. The original
four's working order — refuse, method, aspire, absence — is functional, not a
reenactment of the opus's nigredo → albedo → citrinitas → rubedo sequence; the shared palette
is a label, not a ritual. The full framing, and the single Jung citation for the
whole set, live in the set reader
[`line_set`](https://github.com/docxology/line_set).
